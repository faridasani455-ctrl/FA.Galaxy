Exoplanets

Scientis confirmed more than 6,000 exoplanets, out of the billions that they believe exist. Exoplanets can be grouped based on size, mass, and composition. According to the NASA classifications, the main four types are namely;
1-Gas Giants
2-Neptune-like
3-Super-Earths
4-Terrestrial

Gas giants

Massive planets, often larger than Jupiter and Saturn, composed mostly of hydrogen and helium with thick atmospheres. Some, known as hot Jupiter, orbit incredibly close to their stars.

Neptune-like


Similar in size to Neptune or Uranus, these planets have thick, hydrogen-rich atmospheres with cores of rock and ice.

Super-Earths

Similar in size to Neptune or Uranus, these planets have thick, hydrogen-rich atmospheres with cores of rock and ice.

Terrestrial

Earth-sized or smaller planets composed mainly of rock, silicate, water, or carbon. These are the most difficult to detect due to their small size
